## Historical Background
Villa San Remigio stands on Castagnola hill in Pallanza overlooking Lake Maggiore, an area that became popular with aristocrats during the 19th century. After 1860, two prominent families—the Irish Anglican Brownes and the Neapolitan Della Valle di Casanova—established residences here. The property began as a Swiss chalet built in 1863 by architect Pompeo Azari. After their marriage, the Della Valle couple created elaborate Italian gardens around the chalet, then transformed the building itself into a neo-Renaissance villa with Florentine and Lombard influences. Completed in 1916 with the addition of the "Arms Hall," "Music Room," and "Dining Room," the villa represents an excellent example of late 19th-century aristocratic architecture. The gardens opened to the public in 1929, and in 1977, the property was transferred to the Piedmont Region, which has preserved much of its original character despite some conversions to office space.

## Restoration
The restoration project for Villa San Remigio aims to revitalize this historic property while preserving its architectural and cultural significance. The initiative focuses on transforming the basement and "noble" ground floor for immediate hospitality functions, including receptions, weddings, conferences, and various events.

The basement level will be reconfigured to create reception spaces, catering facilities, and service areas, with ventilated crawl spaces and new wooden flooring installed throughout. This floor will also receive new restroom facilities, including accessible options, along with a bar service area and kitchen facilities for staff.
![sr plan1](/assets/img/projects/2015-arc-it-sr/2015-arc-it-sr-pln1.svg "sr plan1")

On the "noble" floor, the project emphasizes careful restoration of the original "seminato" and wooden flooring, preservation of wall and vault decorations, and improved technical installations like heating and lighting. The "Gothic" room will become a reception area with bar service, while the "Arms Hall" will be optimized for ceremonies and events, taking advantage of its direct outdoor access. The "Music Room" will receive acoustic improvements for concerts and conferences.
![sr plan2](/assets/img/projects/2015-arc-it-sr/2015-arc-it-sr-pln2.svg "sr plan2")

The roof will be checked to asess the condition of roof tiles, replacing damaged ones, ensuring proper water runoff, and addressing any issues with the metal flashing and gutter system.
![sr plan3](/assets/img/projects/2015-arc-it-sr/2015-arc-it-sr-pln3.svg "sr plan3")

![sr ele1](/assets/img/projects/2015-arc-it-sr/2015-arc-it-sr-ele1.svg "sr ele1")

![sr ele2](/assets/img/projects/2015-arc-it-sr/2015-arc-it-sr-ele2.svg "sr ele2")

Throughout the villa, accessibility is a key concern, with the restoration of the existing elevator, installation of new stair lifts, and careful routing to ensure all spaces can be accessed by disabled visitors. Safety enhancements include discrete metal barriers near existing balustrades, consolidation of unstable elements, and specific interventions on the second-floor loggia.
![sr cut1](/assets/img/projects/2015-arc-it-sr/2015-arc-it-sr-cut1.svg "sr cut1")

![sr cut2](/assets/img/projects/2015-arc-it-sr/2015-arc-it-sr-cut2.svg "sr cut2")

The project strikes a balance between historical preservation and practical adaptation, creating a viable venue for hospitality events that can generate immediate economic returns before a future transition to museum or exhibition use.
